How it works

The same idea as classic nail-and-thread string art — computed by an algorithm and printed by your machine.

🖼️

1 · The solver reads your photo

Your image is converted to a brightness map, entirely in your browser. A greedy algorithm then repeatedly finds the single chord between two pins that best darkens the image, thousands of times.

🧵

2 · One continuous string

Each chord starts where the last one ended, so the whole portrait is one unbroken line — exactly like winding a single thread around nails, and perfect for continuous extrusion.

🖨️

3 · Your printer draws it in filament

The G-code prints a solid frame first, then extrudes each string as a fast, taut bridge across the opening — layer by layer — and locks the ends under the frame wall. The result is a rigid one-piece wall art.

FAQ

What photos work best?
One clear subject — a face or a pet — filling most of the frame, with good contrast between subject and background. Bump the contrast slider if the preview looks washed out. Busy group shots need more pins, more strings and a larger print.
Which file should I download?
G-code is the closest to “click print”: it prints the frame, then draws every string as a single taut extrusion bridge — something slicers can't do from a mesh. STL is safer if you want to use your own slicer profile. SVG/PNG are for 2D uses, and the thread pattern is for real nail-and-thread builds.
Is the G-code safe for my printer?
It uses conservative, standard Marlin-flavour commands (G28 home, absolute XYZ, relative extrusion, PLA temps) and respects the selected bed size. Still: preview it in a G-code viewer, and watch the first layers. If in doubt, use the STL export with your own slicer instead.
What material and nozzle?
PLA with a 0.4 mm nozzle. Strings print as ~0.4 mm wide bridges at high speed with full part-cooling, which keeps them taut and clean. Dark filament on a light wall (or inverted + white filament) reads best.
How long does a print take?
A 160 mm standard-detail portrait is typically 1–3 hours depending on your printer's bridge speed. The estimate shown under the preview updates with your settings.
Do my photos get uploaded?
No. Everything — decoding, solving, file generation — happens in your browser. You can even save this page and run it offline.
Can I sell prints I make with this?
Yes. Patterns you generate here are yours, for personal or commercial use, provided you have the rights to the source photo.
